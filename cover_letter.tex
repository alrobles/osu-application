In this letter, I express my interest in the selection process for a tenure-track assistant professor position  at Oregon State University. I completed my Ph.D. in Evolutionary Biology at Arizona State University and defended my dissertation in July 2025. The primary objective of my doctoral research was the development and implementation of new methodologies for both modeling parasite-host interactions and quantifying host susceptibility in the wild to potential, unobserved parasites. My research is based on a powerful intersection of machine learning, high-performance computing, and ecological modeling. I am currently a postdoctoral researcher at the Kansas Biological Survey at the University of Kansas, under the supervision of Dr. Daniel Reuman, where I develop species distribution algorithms based on demographic models.

My research aligns closely with the needs of the Department of Integrative Biology at Oregon State University. After two years as a Senior Data Analyst in the steel industry, where I developed machine learning models to optimize industrial processes, I returned to academia, where I applied my quantitative skills to ecological issues of global importance. That decision led me to pursue a master's degree in physics and, subsequently, a Ph.D. in evolutionary biology, where I modeled host-pathogen interactions at continental and global scales. I later expanded my line of research in my current postdoctoral position, incorporating both theoretical developments and high-performance computing into my current project. Each stage contributed to the development of the set of mathematical and computational tools that I now use in my research on problems in disease ecology, as well as in biogeography and evolutionary biology.

As a computational ecologist, my research is based entirely on publicly available data—primarily data from GBIF, GenBank and climate information, as well as on the open-source, accessible, and replicable code I have developed throughout my research career.
